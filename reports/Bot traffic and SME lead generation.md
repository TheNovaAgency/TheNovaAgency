# How Nova Wins the Bot-Majority Web

By Cloudflare's count, bots now request more web pages than people do. In June 2026, automated traffic made up **about 57% of HTML page requests on Cloudflare's network**, and people made up about 43%. The share still stood at about 57.4% in mid-September. Matthew Prince's "Welp, that happened faster than I predicted" on 3 June 2026 marked a crossover he had forecast for 2027. He put it down to **agentic traffic**: AI assistants and agent browsers that fetch dozens or thousands of pages to answer one person's question. AI training crawlers and conventional bots add to it. **"It's Gemini's fault" does not hold up.** No source puts the crossover down to Gemini. Meta and OpenAI generate most agentic traffic, and Googlebot, though the biggest single crawler, still sends visitors back at roughly 5 crawls per referral. Google's AI does matter in a different way: AI Overviews cut human clicks to websites by roughly 35–60%, which shrinks the human side of the ratio. For UK small and mid-sized businesses (SMEs), the practical result is that buyers increasingly reach a decision before they visit a website. Machines draw up the shortlist, people still verify it, and the businesses that earn genuine third-party mentions, reviews, solid search fundamentals and clean lead data get chosen. For The Nova Agency, the opening is to sell "found by machines, chosen by people, owned by a named human." That means an expanded free check, a WordPress care plan that makes sites ready for AI agents, a clean-leads data offer built on Solo AI, and a follow-up crew with human approval. It should be backed by evidence from the five pilot clients and kept away from the llms.txt and "guaranteed citation" hype that most of the market is selling.

## Bots passed 57% of Cloudflare's page requests a year ahead of forecast

The post is real and widely reported. On **3 June 2026** Prince wrote on X that he "thought it would be end of 2027, then early 2027, but agentic traffic growing so fast that bots have now passed human traffic online for the first time in the Internet's history" ([Yahoo Tech/Tom's Hardware](https://tech.yahoo.com/cybersecurity/articles/bots-now-passed-human-traffic-113500720.html); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/04/ai-agent-web-traffic-surpassed-humans-lending-weight-dead-internet-theory/)). In March at SXSW he had said "in 2027 the amount of bot traffic online will exceed the amount of human traffic." He illustrated it with a shopper who might visit five camera websites while their agent "will often go to 1,000 times the number of sites… So it might go to 5,000 sites" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/19/online-bot-traffic-will-exceed-human-traffic-by-2027-cloudflare-ceo-says/)). Prince himself called the data "a bit messy" and said the exact crossover date is unclear. One report dates the sampled crossover to about **27 April 2026** ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/cloudflare-bots-webpage-requests-479608)). The headline figures cluster tightly: **57.3–57.5% automated**, depending on the window ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317877/20260605/bot-traffic-passes-humans-online-cloudflare-says-agentic-ai-drove-575-share.htm); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/bot-web-traffic-overtaken-human-web-traffic-data-shows-rcna348522)).

The denominator matters, and most coverage skipped it. Radar counts only **HTML page requests on sites that use Cloudflare**. It excludes images, APIs and scripts. It classifies a request as automated when a machine-learning bot score falls between 1 and 29 ([Cloudflare Radar glossary](https://developers.cloudflare.com/radar/glossary/); [Cloudflare bot scores](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/concepts/bot-score/)). So it is not "all internet traffic," and it is not "AI agents" either. It includes search crawlers, uptime monitors, scrapers and malicious bots. The crossover was also gradual. Cloudflare's own 2025 Year in Review already had humans at or below about 47% of HTML requests ([Cloudflare 2025 YIR](https://blog.cloudflare.com/radar-2025-year-in-review/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/bots-now-outnumber-humans-on-the-web-and-most-arent-here-to-search/)). The June reading pushed an existing trend past 50%. The error probably runs towards undercounting bots, because agent browsers running inside ordinary Chrome can score as human ([Botcrawl](https://botcrawl.com/cloudflare-bot-human-traffic/)).

Other vendors, each measuring its own customers, agree on direction but not on level. **Imperva/Thales reported 51% automated traffic for 2024 and over 53% for 2025**, with bad bots alone at about 40% of all traffic ([Thales](https://cpl.thalesgroup.com/about-us/newsroom/2025-imperva-bad-bot-report-ai-internet-traffic); [Imperva 2026](https://www.imperva.com/blog/bad-bot-report-2026-bots-agentic-age/)). HUMAN Security found automated traffic growing **8x faster than human traffic** in 2025, and agentic AI traffic up 7,851% ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/09/3270682/0/en/human-security-s-2026-state-of-ai-traffic-cyberthreat-benchmark-report-signals-a-new-internet-era-automation-growth-now-outpaces-humans.html)). DataDome reports bad-bot traffic growing about 9x faster than human traffic ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/22/datadome-report-finds-bad-bot-traffic-growing-nine-times-faster-than-human-traffic/)). Cloudflare said in October that AI agent daily requests had grown **more than 1,700% in a year** ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jonmarkman/2026/10/01/cloudflare-says-ai-agent-traffic-grew-more-than-1700-in-the-past-year/)). The fair reading is that "first time in history" is a Cloudflare-specific framing of a trend several vendors had already reported. The trend itself is solid.

## Agents and AI crawlers tipped the balance, and Gemini mostly stood aside

The bot share is built in layers. The base is conventional automation: search crawlers, monitors, scrapers and attack bots. Prince puts this at about 20% of traffic before generative AI. That is his estimate, not a published measurement ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/19/online-bot-traffic-will-exceed-human-traffic-by-2027-cloudflare-ceo-says/)). On top of it sit AI training crawlers, which were **80% of AI crawling** in Cloudflare's August 2025 analysis ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/crawlers-click-ai-bots-training)). The fastest-growing layer is retrieval and agent traffic: bots fetching pages live to answer a specific user. TollBit measured retrieval-bot traffic **up 33%** in Q4 2025 while training crawls fell, and AI bot visits rose from 1 per 200 human visits to **1 per 31** within 2025 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/02/04/ai-bot-traffic-closing-in-on-human-web-visits-study-finds/4921090)). HUMAN reports that by October 2026 **Meta and OpenAI accounted for about 90% of agentic traffic**, which was running at about 2.6x September's level ([HUMAN, Oct 2026](https://www.humansecurity.com/learn/blog/october-2026-agentic-traffic/)). DataDome's AI-crawler mix is similar: Meta 46.3%, OpenAI 34.6% ([DataDome](https://datadome.co/threat-research/state-of-bot-and-agent-security-report-key-ai-findings/)).

Google's role is real but it is not the one the "Gemini" guess assumes. Googlebot is the largest single crawler. It made up **4.5% of all HTML requests in 2025**, more than every AI bot combined at 4.2%, and over 28% of verified-bot traffic ([Cloudflare 2025 YIR](https://blog.cloudflare.com/radar-2025-year-in-review/)). Cloudflare criticises Google for running one crawler for both search and AI, so publishers cannot accept one use without the other ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/agentic-internet-bot-report/)). But Google still sends visitors back at a low ratio: about **3:1 at the start of 2025, peaking near 30:1 in April**, and reported by aggregators at about 5:1 through 2026. Compare that with ratios in the hundreds or thousands for Anthropic, OpenAI and Perplexity ([Cloudflare 2025 YIR](https://blog.cloudflare.com/radar-2025-year-in-review/)). Coverage of the June milestone names agents, not Gemini, as the driver ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/cloudflare-bots-webpage-requests-479608)). Prince's best-known Gemini comment is from July 2025 and concerns blocking Google's AI features, not traffic volume ([X @eastdakota](https://x.com/eastdakota/status/1940834337022517456)).

There is a more interesting sense in which Google's AI does shift the ratio, through the human side. A bot share rises when bot requests grow *or* human requests shrink, and human clicks are shrinking. Google AI Overviews cut clicks on the top organic result by about **58%** in Ahrefs' data ([Business Wire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20260518322756/en/New-Research-Googles-AI-Overviews-Now-Cost-Websites-58-of-Their-Clicks)). A field study using causal methods put the cut at **38–40%** ([Agarwal & Sen](https://www.tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/TSE/documents/sem2026/eco_platforms/agarwal_google_search_aio.pdf)). Gemini is also gaining fast as a destination: its share of visits to generative-AI sites rose from under 9% to about **27–28%** between June 2025 and May 2026, while ChatGPT's fell from about 76% to about 53% ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/chatgpts-lead-shrinks-as-gemini-surges-in-ai-traffic-war/)). So the accurate answer is this. Gemini did not create the bot majority, but Google's AI answers are one reason fewer humans click through, and Gemini is becoming a discovery surface SMEs must appear on. One coincidence is useful for content: on the **same day as Prince's post, 3 June 2026**, the UK CMA imposed conduct requirements on Google. These let publishers opt out of AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini without losing search ranking ([Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/06/03/cma-imposes-google-publisher-ai-opt-out/)). For an SME that wants to be recommended, opting out would usually defeat the purpose.

## SMEs are losing clicks to AI answers, but they can still win customers by making the AI shortlist

The bot majority is not a crisis for a plumber in Chelmsford or a B2B firm in Basildon. **The question is where buying decisions now happen.** The evidence is consistent that informational queries lose the most traffic. Seer found AI Overviews triggered on **36% of informational queries but only 5% of transactional ones** ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ctr-recovery-study-475566)). Zero-click searches rose to about **68% of US Google searches** in early 2026, though the data source changed in the meantime and the comparison with 2024 is approximate ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/)). In UK evidence submitted to the CMA, AI Overviews cut news click-through by 47.5% on desktop ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/digital-journalism/google-ai-overviews-publishers-report-clickthroughs-authoritas-report/)). So an SME whose leads came from "how much does a loft conversion cost" blog posts is exposed. One whose leads come from "loft conversion Chelmsford" money pages, the Google Business Profile and referrals is much less exposed.

The bigger change is the shortlist. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, **45% of consumers used AI tools for local business recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier**, while Google's share of local discovery fell from 83% to 71% ([DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/ai-local-business-discovery-website-strategy)). That sample is US-weighted, and no UK equivalent was found. AI engines name very few businesses. SOCi found ChatGPT recommends just **1.2% of local business locations** ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/ai-search-recommends-only-12-local-businesses-rest-are-invisible)), and AI Overviews return the fewest recommendations of any engine ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-ai-visibility-study/)). In B2B, Forrester found **94% of buyers used generative AI in their last purchase**, and G2 found 51% of software buyers now start research in a chatbot more often than in Google ([Grey Matter](https://gogreymatter.com/blog/how-b2b-buyers-use-ai-to-choose-vendors/); [Silvengate](https://silvengate.com/news/buyers-ask-ai-which-software-to-trust/)). Gartner's lower 45% shows how much definitions matter ([AuthorityTech](https://authoritytech.io/curated/gartner-rep-free-buyers-ai-sales-rep-2026)). Humans have not left the process. **88% of consumers who use AI for local recommendations say they verify what it tells them** ([Bill Hartzer](https://www.billhartzer.com/?p=18508)), and 69% of B2B buyers want to check AI insights with a salesperson ([AuthorityTech](https://authoritytech.io/curated/gartner-rep-free-buyers-ai-sales-rep-2026)). The decisive moment has moved upstream. A business absent from the AI shortlist never gets the verification visit.

AI referrals are real but still small. Similarweb counts about **770.7 million AI-referred visits a month worldwide**, more than double the year before ([Similarweb](https://aisearch.similarweb.com/blog/ai-referral-traffic-by-industry/)). Conversion evidence is genuinely mixed. For B2B, the picture is favourable: Ahrefs' AI visitors were 0.5% of traffic but 12.1% of signups ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/llm-search)). For ecommerce it is unfavourable: a *Marketing Science* paper found ChatGPT referrals converting worse than Google search ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/full/10.1287/mksc.2025.0489)). The honest pitch is "fewer, often better-qualified visitors, and much influence you cannot see." It is not "AI traffic converts 10x."

Two operational problems sit underneath this, and both are easy to sell because owners can feel them. The first is **measurement corruption**. AI agents running in standard Chrome get past GA4's bot filter and show up as human sessions ([MeasureU](https://measureu.com/dead-internet-ga4)). Form-filling bots fill CRMs with fake leads that distort cost-per-lead, and they also feed false conversions back to Google and Meta bidding ([Managed Nerds](https://www.managednerds.tech/blog/ai-spam-leads-are-exploding-how-to-stop-fake-form-fills-and-bot-calls)). The second is **accidental invisibility**. Since 1 July 2025, Cloudflare has defaulted new domains towards blocking AI crawlers, and its managed robots.txt signals ai-train=no ([BrandAxis](https://brandaxis.ai/blog/cloudflare-blocking-ai-crawlers/); [Search Engine World](https://www.searchengineworld.com/cloudflare-enters-the-robots-txt-fray-with-a-content-signals-policy-for-ai-bots)). Sources disagree on whether those defaults also catch AI *search* bots ([Green Wire Media](https://greenwiremedia.com/blog/cloudflare-ai-crawlers/)), which is exactly why every SME on Cloudflare should have the settings checked. Agentic *buying* is still mostly hype for UK SMEs. OpenAI pulled standalone Instant Checkout in March 2026 ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/03/06/openai-shifts-checkout-plans-agentic-commerce-strategy/amp/)). Shopify agentic purchasing is US-buyers-only ([Shopify UK](https://www.shopify.com/uk/blog/agentic-commerce)). Visa went live in Europe with Barclays, HSBC UK, Lloyds and NatWest ([Visa UK](https://www.visa.co.uk/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.3457328.html)), yet one fraud vendor counted only **"hundreds" of completed agent transactions worldwide by September 2026** ([Shatale](https://shatale.com/blog/mastercard-agent-pay-visa-intelligent-commerce)). HUMAN also notes that more than 95% of AI-driven traffic goes to retail, media and travel ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/09/3270682/0/en/human-security-s-2026-state-of-ai-traffic-cyberthreat-benchmark-report-signals-a-new-internet-era-automation-growth-now-outpaces-humans.html)). For a local service business in 2026, AI means AI answers about the business, not agents placing orders with it.

## What actually earns AI visibility, and what is snake oil

Google's own guidance, published 15 May 2026, says AI Overviews and AI Mode use the same index and ranking systems as ordinary search, so success "starts with succeeding in search." It names llms.txt, AI-specific Markdown files, content "chunking," rewriting content for AI, and inauthentic brand mentions as unnecessary or unhelpful ([SEJ](https://searchenginejournal.com/googles-new-ai-search-guide-calls-aeo-and-geo-still-seo/575026)). Independent data mostly agrees. Ahrefs' study of 75,000 brands found **branded web mentions correlated 0.664 with AI Overview visibility, against 0.218 for backlinks** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)). The same study found **97% of llms.txt files received zero requests** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/llms-txt-adoption-rises-8-8x-but-97-of-files-get-zero-ai-requests/)). A difference-in-differences test found that adding schema made no significant difference to AI citations ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-marketing/does-structured-data-actually-improve-answer-engine-citations/)). For ChatGPT's local answers, BrightLocal found sources split **58% business websites, 27% business mentions, 15% directories** ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/local-seo/uncovering-chatgpt-search-sources/)). An SME's own site and its reputation elsewhere on the web do most of the work.

## Nova's move: sell "found by machines, chosen by people, owned by a human"

### Positioning that turns the bot majority into a trust argument

Nova's existing line, "AI does the work, people own the result," fits this moment unusually well. When most traffic is machines, three things become scarce: a human who can vouch for what a business says, a human who answers the enquiry, and a human accountable for what the AI did. UK owners are adopting AI quickly. FSB puts small-firm usage at **55%**, while the ONS puts all-firm usage at **26%** ([Business Times](https://business-times.co.uk/articles/technology/ai-adoption-could-unlock-42bn-opportunity-for-small-businesses-says-fsb/); [ONS BICS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/businessservices/bulletins/businessinsightsandimpactontheukeconomy/2april2026)). But they distrust it: **92% have concerns, 54% about inaccurate answers** ([Startups.co.uk](https://startups.co.uk/news/sme-ai-trust-gap/)). Regulators have also settled the accountability question. The CMA's March 2026 guidance says a business is fully responsible for what its AI agents say and do, even if a third party built them, with fines of up to 10% of turnover ([TLT](https://www.tlt.com/insights-and-events/insight/agentic-ai-cma-publishes-guidance-on-consumer-law-and-dmcca-risks)). The ICO says deployers "cannot blame" agents for errors ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/01/22/ico-tech-futures-report-agentic-ai-102mdki)). "Every output has a named human owner" is therefore not just a brand flourish. It answers the most common objection and matches what UK law expects.

The recommended outward positioning is: **"Most of the web is now machines. We make sure the machines find you, and a named person makes sure customers choose you."** Messaging should lead with hours saved and staff augmented, not replaced. Among AI-using SMEs, 95% report no change in headcount, and the main gains are productivity ([ISER](https://www.iser.essex.ac.uk/research/news/2026/03/19/major-jump-in-tech-adoption-as-study-finds-half-of-smes-using-ai-with-limited-headcount-impact-so-far)). Messaging should also name concrete use cases, because "lack of identified need" is the most common barrier in DSIT's research (about 71%, per a secondary summary of [DSIT](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6960f3924343a0da370869ba/AI_Adoption_Research_Report.pdf)). Nova should avoid calling any of this "GEO" in customer-facing copy. Owners ask "why don't I show up when people ask ChatGPT?" and the copy should use their words.

### Five pilots become Essex's first AI-visibility evidence base

The five pilot clients are worth more as an evidence base than as five logos. Nova should baseline each pilot before any changes, ideally before Christmas. The baseline should cover a fixed prompt set across four AI engines, GA4 AI Assistant sessions (forward-only data, so start the custom channel now), Bing AI citations, GBP actions, and *validated* leads by source. The same measures should be repeated at 90 and 180 days. This gives Nova something almost no competitor has: before-and-after numbers it can substantiate. Under CAP rule 3.7, those numbers are the only lawful basis for claims such as "clients appeared in X% more AI answers" ([Bird & Bird](https://mediawrites.twobirds.com/post/102jkyz/the-asas-guidance-on-ai-as-a-marketing-term)). The pilots should span at least one local service trade, one B2B professional service and, if possible, one ecommerce client. The evidence shows these segments behave differently: transactional local queries are less exposed to AI Overviews, B2B AI referrals appear higher-intent, and ecommerce conversion from AI referrals is mixed. Each case study should carry the named human owner of the work, which shows the brand promise in practice.

### Content that rides the news without overclaiming

The strongest content treats the headline sceptically and makes it useful to the reader. A flagship piece titled **"Bots now outnumber humans online. Gemini didn't do it, and here's what it means for your enquiries"** works as a blog post, a LinkedIn post and a short YouTube explainer (YouTube reaches 94% of UK adult internet users for an average 51 minutes a day ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/online-habits/from-apps-to-ai-search-how-the-uk-goes-online-in-2025))). It turns the user's own guess into a hook, explains the 57% denominator honestly, and ends on the free check. The second piece is a practical how-to, **"Is your website hiding from ChatGPT? A five-minute Cloudflare and robots.txt check,"** which leads straight into the paid audit. A third, **"We tested llms.txt so you don't have to,"** builds credibility by debunking a tactic competitors sell. Ahrefs' 97%-zero-requests finding and Google's own guidance support it.

The highest-value asset is proprietary. Nova could publish **"The Essex AI Shortlist,"** a quarterly study that asks the four main AI engines "who's the best [trade] in [Chelmsford, Colchester, Southend, Basildon…]" across about ten sectors. It would report patterns rather than shaming named firms: how few businesses get named, which sources the engines cite, and how often the answers are wrong or out of date. A companion **"Bot weather report"** could use anonymised Cloudflare or server logs from Nova-hosted WordPress sites to show what share of a typical Essex SME site's traffic is human. That answers a question national data leaves open, since HUMAN's data says most AI traffic goes to retail, media and travel rather than local services. Both pieces suit local media and the Essex Chambers of Commerce, which publicised the BCC/ISER AI study run with the University of Essex ([Essex Chambers](https://www.essexchambers.co.uk/half-of-smes-using-ai-with-limited-headcount-impact-so-far/)). A monthly email newsletter carries all of this. Email is the best-evidenced owned channel in the UK, with a 2.3% average unique click rate and 18% for the top decile ([e-shot citing DMA](https://e-shot.net/insights/blog/2025-averages)).

## UK compliance rules double as selling points

Four sets of rules bind both Nova's own marketing and what it builds for clients, and each one supports the "named human owner" pitch. **PECR outreach:** cold email to limited companies and LLPs is lawful with an opt-out and a legitimate-interests basis. Email or text to **sole traders and non-LLP partnerships needs consent or the soft opt-in** ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)), and many Essex trades fall into that group. Under the Data (Use and Access) Act, PECR fines rose to **£17.5m or 4% of turnover from 5 February 2026** ([Blake Morgan](https://www.blakemorgan.co.uk/data-use-and-access-act-2025-privacy-and-electronic-communications-regulations/)). That makes consent capture a non-negotiable feature of every Follow-up Crew automation, including WhatsApp, where 90% of UK online adults can be reached ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/online-habits/from-apps-to-ai-search-how-the-uk-goes-online-in-2025)). One gap: no source in this research confirms how PECR treats WhatsApp Business messages, so Nova should treat them as electronic mail. **Reviews:** under the DMCC Act, fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews have been banned since **6 April 2025**. Businesses that publish reviews need a policy and reasonable steps to prevent banned ones ([CMA208](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)). The CMA opened five investigations in 2026 ([RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/consumer/summer-2026/cma-launches-5-x-investigations-into-fake-and-misleading-review-practices/)), so review requests must go to all customers, never just the happy ones, and any incentive must be disclosed.

**AI agents and data:** the CMA holds businesses fully responsible for their agents' consumer-facing claims ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=27b56295-fcbe-4339-a274-c3c2f6dc6be4)), and the ICO holds deployers responsible for agents' data handling. When Nova builds a Crew or Mod for a client, the client is normally the data controller and Nova the processor. Nova therefore needs Article 28 terms, a sub-processor list naming its LLM vendors and any international transfers, a DPIA template, logs, and human sign-off on significant decisions. Packaged as "compliance included," this answers the 39% of owners worried about security and IP ([National Technology News](https://nationaltechnology.co.uk/FSB_AI_Fears_Preventing_Small_Businesses_From_Adding_42bn_UK_Economy_Annually.php)). **Advertising claims:** the ASA requires documentary evidence for performance claims, and an "AI-generated" label does not cure a misleading ad ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/06/18/asa-warns-on-ai-generated-content-and-deepfakes-102n4o1)). CAP issued guidance on advertising AI assistant products in August 2026 ([LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/news/cap-issues-guidance-on-ai-assistant-advertising-compliance-under-cap-code)). Nova should never promise AI rankings, citations or lead uplifts until the pilots supply the evidence, and it should never present a self-adopted AI policy as an official certification.

## Conclusion

The bot majority is a story about the middle of the funnel, not the top. Machines now do the reading, comparing and shortlisting. Humans still verify and buy, but they visit fewer sites and arrive later, with their minds mostly made up. So a "traffic" KPI now misleads twice: bots and agents inflate sessions, and decisions that never produce a click go uncounted. The SMEs that do well will measure *validated leads and shortlist presence* rather than sessions. They will earn the off-site reputation that AI engines draw on, and they will make the verification visit convert, quickly and with a named human. None of this needs exotic agent standards. It needs disciplined fundamentals plus honest measurement.

That makes Nova's advantage unusually specific. Competitors are racing to sell "GEO" as a new line item, and Google, Bing and GA4 are giving away the measurement for free. Nova can instead sell what neither the platforms nor the hype merchants offer: someone accountable for whether a small business is found, described correctly and followed up properly. A WordPress heritage covers the plumbing. Solo AI and Nova Crew cover the measurement and follow-up. The named-owner rule matches what the CMA and ICO already require. If the five pilots produce honest before-and-after evidence by spring 2027, which is roughly when Google's CMA-mandated changes are due, Nova will be one of very few UK studios that can show, rather than claim, what works in a web where most visitors are machines.
